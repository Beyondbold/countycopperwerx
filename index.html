The maker

Grounded in the work

Every piece of copper already has a story. My job is to give it a new one.
CopperStrength · Energy
WoodWarmth · Ground

County Copper Werx started with a pile of copper pipe that was too good to scrap. Lines pulled out of old houses, leftovers from jobs, fittings that had already done decades of work. Each length came with its own tool marks and color.

Instead of sending it off to be melted down, it gets cut, fitted and sweat-soldered by hand into things you live with: a bench by the door, a table next to the couch, a lit shelf on the wall. The frames are paired with reclaimed and live-edge wood, so every piece balances the cool, bright metal with something warm and natural.

The logo says it best. Two halves in one circle, each carrying a little of the other. That is how the work gets done too: slowly, with a clear head, and with respect for the materials.

How a piece gets made

Slow hands, clear head.

  1. 01

    Salvage

    Copper pipe and fittings are rescued from renovations and job sites before they get scrapped.

  2. 02

    Clean

    Old solder, flux and grime come off. The honest marks of age stay.

  3. 03

    Cut & fit

    Each length is measured, cut and dry-fit until the frame sits square and true.

  4. 04

    Sweat

    Every joint is sweat-soldered with a torch, the way copper has been joined for a century.

  5. 05

    Wood & finish

    Reclaimed or live-edge wood is sanded, torched or stained, then set into the frame.

A living finish

Copper changes with you

Unsealed copper reacts with air, moisture and touch. It darkens from bright pink-orange to bronze and brown, and outdoors it slowly turns the soft green you see on old rooftops.

Every piece can be sealed to hold its shine, or left raw so it ages along with your home. Just say which you'd like when you order.

Nothing alive stays the same color forever.
